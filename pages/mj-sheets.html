Red Coat Underpass

One brief, twelve camera angles. A lone figure in a saturated red coat in an empty concrete underpass at night — hip-height 35mm film still, one overhead sodium streetlight as the only key, wet floor catching thin reflections, deep unlit tunnel falling away behind. Every tile holds the subject, the light, the floor and the tunnel fixed, and moves exactly one thing: the camera.

How the sheet was made

One variable per bullet. The shared head and tail of the brief are byte-identical across all twelve prompts; only the clause between them changes. That is what makes the sheet a comparison rather than twelve unrelated pictures — any difference you see is the camera.

Attribution comes from the feed, not the page. Midjourney's imagine feed is a shared timeline: the account owner's own concurrent jobs sit in it with their own prompts. Matching a rendered card to a prompt by reading the card's text attributed six of the first twelve tiles to the wrong job. Every task id on this sheet was re-derived from the feed's own command string, and only plain 16:9 v8.2 jobs were accepted.

Three angles were re-shot. The overhead crane and the dutch tilt first came back as eye-level and level respectively, and the mirrored-ceiling variant came back as a frontal shot with the reflection in the ceiling — Midjourney did not follow the instruction in any of the three. Each prompt was reworded and re-rendered, and every re-render was checked against the image before it was accepted. A job that "succeeded" is not evidence that the angle landed.
