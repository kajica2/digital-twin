# Two A.M. Infrastructure

> Mood-story prose (the concept, kept verbatim):
>
> The ferry terminal at two in the morning is a motherboard under a fist of
> rain. Every sign a capacitor, every window a cooling fin, the harbor
> breathing in and out against the seawall. A delivery rider crosses the
> plaza at exactly the speed the rain allows no faster, no slower, a packet
> routed through a city that has stopped asking where anything belongs.
> Above it all, one green light on the far shore: not a message, just a
> pulse. The city doesn't text. It idles. He stands at the railing long
> enough to stop being a tourist and start being infrastructure himself.
> There's a comfort in that, the way there's comfort in any machine that
> runs all night you don't have to be happy, you just have to hold your
> frequency. Rain keeps coming. The green light keeps pulsing. The city
> keeps not needing to know his name.
