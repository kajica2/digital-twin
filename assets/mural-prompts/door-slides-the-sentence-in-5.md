song-note: >
  "Door I didn't open slides the rest of the sentence in" — one beat of
  "Breath Forgets the Count", locked into a tighter form.

  WHY THIS SHAPE: the twelve-frame set is the WIDE read of the lyric —
  twelve different scenes, one per beat. A single beat told tightly is a
  different animal: the same scene, the same man, the same light, at five
  distances. The repo's own camera test (one-door-five-ways) proved the
  distance clause only lands when it is written as WHAT IS IN FRAME and
  what is not, so every bullet here names the door, the light band, the
  coat and the floor — and the camera's place as a location in the room.

  THE LOOK: the master's — handmade 2D bedtime-animation still, linocut
  print texture, deep blue and warm red ink on cream paper, no text.

  THE IDENTITY: the master's --edit attachment is today's, so the frames
  are edit jobs and the artist's own face is the anchor. The prose repeats
  the identity in every bullet and denies the wardrobe that out-ranks it
  in edit mode. The door opens by itself — the absent person is the
  sentence that came through it, never a second face.

  THE FIVE: the wide (the room and the man), the middle (the man and the
  door), the near (the man at the threshold), the close (the light band on
  the floor and his hand), and the held (the door open, the room quiet,
  him standing inside the light).

  NOTE ON STRUCTURE: a blockquote beside a bullet does NOT survive the
  pack parser — the label is folded into the prompt that follows it, and
  Midjourney refuses the whole thing. The frame labels live in the
  frontmatter below instead.
