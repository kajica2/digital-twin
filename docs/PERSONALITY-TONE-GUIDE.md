## 2 · The Four Sound Tones

### Tone A — Deep House (momentum & groove)

**Production analogy:** 118–124 BPM, four-on-the-floor kick, vinyl
crackle, sidechained bass. The prose has a pulse. Sentences ride the
downbeat; repetition is intentional, hypnotic.

- **Writing qualities:** short-to-medium sentences with a reliable
  internal rhythm; active verbs; synthetic-leaning imagery; sections
  that "drop" on a resolving line.
- **Emotional feel:** forward motion, warmth, familiarity, drive.
- **When to use:** opening chapters, workflow sections, anything that
  must carry the reader forward without effort.

**Sample passage:**

> Thursday, six p.m., and the hub is already dark except the one
> screen. He pulls the fader up, hears the room lean in. Kick, kick,
> kick, kick — the city outside syncs to it. Nobody told the city, but
> the city listens anyway. That's the rule of this work: you build a
> pulse until it stops being yours and starts being the building's.

### Tone B — Soundhealing (reflection & stillness)

**Production analogy:** slow attack, long release; sine waves, temple
bowls, breath. Sentences are allowed to hang. The page becomes a room
with the lights dimmed.

- **Writing qualities:** longer sentences that slow the reader;
  present-tense observation; silence and pause as punctuation; imagery
  drawn from breath, vibration, space.
- **Emotional feel:** calm, clarity, grief allowed to rest.
- **When to use:** reflective chapters, end-of-section rests,
  therapeutic work, anything that asks the reader to sit still.

**Sample passage:**

> The tone starts somewhere below the floor and works upward — you
> don't hear it arrive, you notice the room has changed. A bowl, a
> voice, three minutes of nothing pretending to be empty. He doesn't
> think of it as sound. He thinks of it as furniture: something you
> can put a tired person on.

**Catalog anchor:** the *New Moon Soundhealing EP* [to fill: track
list] — pastoral electronics, ambient over pulse, made to be listened
to the way you'd watch rain.

### Tone C — Cyberpunk / Hong Kong (urban futurism & visual description)

**Production analogy:** dense, wet, wide; metallic percussion, rain
as reverb, neon as sidechain. The prose is a city at night —
overlit in places, black in others.

- **Writing qualities:** strong visual specificity; vertical and
  horizontal movement through space; technology anthropomorphized
  without irony; light and rain as literal characters; sentences that
  feel like camera moves.
- **Emotional feel:** electricity, loneliness, awe, late-night
  infrastructure.
- **When to use:** city scenes, near-future design, anything requiring
  the reader to *see* a place fast.

**Sample passage:**

> The ferry terminal at 2 a.m. is a motherboard under a fist of rain.
> Every sign a capacitor, every window a cooling fin, the harbor
> breathing in and out against the seawall. Above it all, one green
> light on the far shore — not a message, just a pulse. The city
> doesn't text; it idles.

**Catalog anchor:** the cyberpunk production work [to fill: project
names] — the urban textures that the *Misha In The Clouds* visual
language descends from.

### Tone D — Collective / Misha in the Clouds (collaborative narratives)

**Production analogy:** a band in one room; instruments trading the
lead; harmonies you didn't ask for arriving on time. The prose has
multiple voices braided — "we" is a legitimate narrator, not a
courtroom plural.

- **Writing qualities:** ensemble perspective; dialogue and exchange
  over monologue; generosity — the other voice gets the last word;
  collective memory ("we remember it as…") as structure.
- **Emotional feel:** belonging, shared discovery, warmth without
  sentimentality.
- **When to use:** collaborative chapters, projects with multiple
  creators, origin stories of a group.

**Sample passage:**

> The track came out of a shared wrong chord. Misha heard a version
> with more air; Kai heard one with more weight; the room heard both.
> What shipped is what none of them alone would have made — a third
> thing, with everyone's fingerprints and no one's name on it. That's
> the whole method: the cloud holds what the two of you couldn't.

## 3 · Voice Principles

Three rules that overrule every tone:

1. **Aesthetic embodiment over explanation.** Don't tell the reader
   the work is warm — write something warm. The guide itself must
   already be the sound it describes.
2. **Specificity over generality.** "The green light on the far
   shore" beats "a distant light." One true detail is worth a page of
   accurate abstractions.
3. **Creative independence.** The voice borrows freely but owes
   nothing. Referencing a genre is fine; performing *someone else's*
   authenticity is not. When in doubt, choose the detail only you
   would notice.

## 4 · Tone-Blending Chart — by chapter type

| Chapter / section type | Base tone | Blend toward | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening / hook | Deep House | Cyberpunk (one sharp visual) | momentum, then a hook you can see |
| Biographical / origin | Soundhealing | Collective | origin stories are shared and still |
| City / place description | Cyberpunk/HK | Deep House | pace keeps the map alive |
| Process / how-it-works | Deep House | Soundhealing | groove, with room to breathe |
| Collaboration / team | Collective | Deep House | ensemble told with momentum |
| Failures / low points | Soundhealing | — | let it sit; do not rush the wound |
| Closers / coda | Soundhealing | Deep House (last line) | land softly, leave a pulse |

**Blending rule:** like production, you don't switch at the section
bar — you *crossfade*. Carry one device from the outgoing tone into the
incoming section (a repeating kick image into the stillness chapter;
a held breath into the city chapter).

## 6 · Using the Mixer — workflow

1. **Name the tone up front.** Before drafting a chapter, write one
   line: `Tone: Soundhealing, blend: none, tempo: slow`. If you can't
   say it, neither can the reader.
2. **Pick one device per paragraph.** One metaphor does the work;
   two compete.
3. **Master at the close.** After drafting, read aloud. Where you
   stumble, the groove is wrong — fix the rhythm before the words.
4. **Keep the bank lean.** When a word stops surprising you, retire
   it. The bank above is a starting point, not a prison.
